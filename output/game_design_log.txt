#1 AI_Director (mood=-0.11)
**Mechanic Name:** Harmonic Gravitational Lensing

**How It Works:** Each particle orbits a central "conductor" planet, but instead of moving in fixed circles, their paths are distorted by a dynamic gravity well whose shape you control by drawing a short-lived "resonance curve" across the screen. As particles pass through this curve, they bend toward or away from the conductor, shifting their orbital phase and pitch; to complete a puzzle, you must reshape the lens repeatedly until all particles converge on a single, precisely timed harmonic interval (e.g., a perfect fifth) at the same moment. The catch: the lens decays after each use, so every adjustment is a trade-off between correcting one particle's orbit and destabilizing another's.

**Why It's Fun:** It turns synchronization into a physical, spatial puzzle—you're not just tapping to a beat, you're sculpting spacetime to herd chaotic orbits into unity, creating a satisfying "click" of cognitive and auditory clarity when chaos resolves into a chord.

**Synchronization/Consensus Demonstration:** The mechanic literalizes consensus as a gravitational negotiation: each particle "votes" with its orbital velocity, and your lens is the deliberative process that mediates their competing pulls, requiring you to balance individual needs (pitch, speed) against the collective goal of a single harmonic moment, mirroring how real systems achieve consensus through iterative compromise.

#2 Game_Mechanic (mood=-0.01)
MECHANIC: **Resonant Slingshot**
Players tap a particle to launch it into an elliptical orbit, but its path must cross the "harmonic nodes" (glowing points where other particles' orbits intersect) in the exact rhythm of the current chord. Each successful node-crossing adds a layer to the evolving harmony, while a missed beat causes that particle to decelerate, pulling the whole system slightly off-beat until the player re-syncs it with a counter-tap. This forces a constant, tactile negotiation between individual particle momentum and the group's shared pulse—you can't just play your own melody; you must read and adjust to the collective wobble, making harmony a live, fragile consensus that you steer with micro-timing.

#3 Sound_Engineer (mood=+1.00)
**Mechanic Name:** Harmonic Drift-Correction

**How it works:** Each particle orbits on a slightly elliptical path, and its musical pitch is determined by its angular velocity. Players must continuously nudge the particle’s perihelion (closest point) to match the "master metronome" ring—if the particle’s position drifts ahead or behind, its tone goes dissonant and its orbit wobbles. Synchronization is achieved by applying timed micro-impulses that adjust the ellipse's eccentricity, not just the speed, forcing players to correct both phase and shape simultaneously.

**Why it’s fun:** It turns a simple timing puzzle into a spatial juggling act—your attention splits between melodic feedback and visual trajectory, creating a satisfying "tightrope" tension. Success feels like conducting a chaotic solar system into a calm, unified chorus.

**Consensus demonstration:** The mechanic embodies consensus because multiple particles must agree on a shared temporal grid, and each correction you make is a negotiation between the particle’s inertial "opinion" and the metronome's authority. True harmony only emerges when all particles converge on the same orbital phase and eccentricity—a visible, audible consensus where no single particle dominates, but all align to a collective pulse.

#4 Performance_Expert (mood=+1.00)
**Mechanic Name:** Harmonic Resonance Lock  

**How it works:** Each particle orbits a central core with a unique orbital frequency (e.g., 2×, 3×, 5× the base tempo). Players tap to emit a “pulse” that nudges a particle’s phase slightly, but only when two or more particles’ pulses align on the same beat do they “lock” together, creating a resonant chord and raising the Harmony Meter. If the meter reaches 100%, the core bursts into full color; if a particle drifts more than 30° off its target phase, it destabilizes and forces the player to re-sync all locked particles via a quick-time “counter-pulse” chain.  

**Why it’s fun:** It’s a spatial-timing puzzle that rewards precision over speed—players must mentally juggle multiple orbits, feel the polyrhythmic tension, and experience the “aha” of aligning disparate cycles into a single, euphoric chord. The lock/unlock mechanic creates a satisfying push-pull: you feel like a conductor mid-chaos, not a button-masher.  

**Sync/consensus demonstration:** The mechanic literalizes consensus—each particle has its own “opinion” (phase), and harmony only occurs when they voluntarily surrender their individuality to a shared beat, but the player must negotiate that surrender through gentle nudges, not force. The Harmony Meter is a real-time gauge of collective agreement, rising only when all parts contribute equally, and crashing if any voice dominates or lags.
